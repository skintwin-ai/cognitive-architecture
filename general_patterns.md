# General Patterns from Cognitive Architecture Framework

## System Structure Patterns

### System 4 (9 Terms)
- **Centers**: 4 primary centers
  - C₁: Idea - Creative energies, electronic processes in neurons
  - C₂: Knowledge - Conscious energies, synaptic interconnections
  - C₃: Routine - Sensitive energies, gross body processes
  - C₄: Form - Vital energies, physical form in environment

- **Terms**: 9 total terms with specific relationships
  - T₁: Perception - Emotional feeling of need/capacity to act
  - T₂: Creation - Generation of ideas
  - T₃: Transfer - Movement between knowledge and routine
  - T₄: Organization - Processing of sensory input
  - T₅: Action - Physical behavior
  - T₆: Corporeal - Physical manifestation
  - T₇: Memory/Resource Capacity - Storage of experience
  - T₈: Balance - Equilibrium between motor output and sensory input
  - T₉: Hierarchy - Ordered relationship of centers

- **Transform Sequence**: 1→4→2→8→5→7 repeating
- **Modes**: Expressive and Regenerative
- **Cycles**: 12 steps in 3 cycles

### System 5 (20 Terms)
- **Centers**: 5 active centers
  - C₁: Idea - Higher abstraction level
  - C₂: Knowledge (cerebral) - Conscious knowledge
  - C₃: Knowledge (limbic) - Emotional knowledge
  - C₄: Routine - Procedural processes
  - C₅: Form - Physical manifestation

- **Virtual Images**: First appear in System 5, affecting 3 terms
- **Integration**: Autonomic and somatic nervous systems

## Dimensional Patterns

### Three Polar Dimensions of Human Behavior
1. **Potential Dimension** (T₂-T₇ polarity)
   - Creation of idea (T₂) relates to resource capacity (T₇)
   - Focused through intuitive right hemisphere
   - Sterile without complementary pole

2. **Commitment Dimension** (T₅-T₄ polarity)
   - Physical action (T₅) relates to organization of sensory input (T₄)
   - Focused through left hemisphere
   - Concerned with explicit techniques of behavior with language

3. **Performance Dimension** (T₈-T₁ polarity)
   - Balance of motor output with sensory input (T₈) relates to perceived response capacity (T₁)
   - Evaluates performance against expectations
   - Mediated through autonomic nervous system

## Evolutionary Patterns

### Evolutionary vs. Involutionary Variants
- **Evolutionary Variant**: Progressively integrates experience toward timeless understanding
  - Development begins at Form level (C₄) and proceeds upward
  - Plants → Invertebrates → Vertebrates → Humans
  - From vital energies to creative energies

- **Involutionary Variant**: Leads to decay and fragmentation
  - In System 3: Universal Set links Centers in reverse order
  - In System 4: Particular Centers 3 and 4 exchange places
  - Forms become ends in themselves, values inverted
  - Can feed on Evolutionary Variant

### Evolutionary Hierarchy
1. **Form Level (C₄)**: Exploration of static form (eukaryotic cell by plants)
2. **Routine Level (C₃)**: Exploration of sensory-motor mechanisms (invertebrates)
3. **Knowledge Level (C₂)**: Exploration of conscious patterns of emotive behavior (vertebrates)
4. **Idea Level (C₁)**: Creative abilities associated with language (humans)

## Hierarchical Patterns

### Discretionary Hierarchy of Human Behavior
1. **Electronic Processes (C₁)**: Generate human ideas in neurons of CNS
2. **Synaptic Interconnections (C₂)**: Represent invested knowledge from cultural/genetic/evolutionary history
3. **Gross Body Processes (C₃)**: Transmit through peripheral nervous system to muscles and organs
4. **Physical Form (C₄)**: Altered by animated action sequences in relation to environment

### Universal and Particular Sets
- **Universal Sets**: Primary and Secondary sets that provide context
- **Particular Sets**: Three sets that interact with Universal Sets
- **Coherence**: Integration between Universal and Particular Sets
